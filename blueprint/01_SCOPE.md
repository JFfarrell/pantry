# Project: Pantry

## Problem Statement

Cooking from recipes found online is a chain of manual, error-prone steps that nobody completes reliably. A recipe worth keeping lives on a web page cluttered with ads and life stories; saving it means a browser bookmark that rots, a screenshot, or retyping it. Deciding what to cook means holding half-remembered recipes in your head with no view of how long each takes, how much energy it provides, or what is actually in season. Turning those choices into a shop means reading every recipe again, hand-merging overlapping ingredients, mentally scaling quantities when the recipe serves four and you are feeding six, and then typing thirty-odd items into a supermarket site one at a time. Each step loses information from the one before it, so the usual outcome is that the attempt is abandoned partway: people fall back on the same handful of meals, shop without a list, and buy food that does not correspond to anything they intend to cook.

The people affected are home cooks who want to cook deliberately from recipes they have chosen rather than improvise — in the first instance the developer building this, a single user on a single Android device. Existing recipe apps solve fragments of this and charge for the rest: most require an account and a cloud backend, many gate shopping-list export behind a subscription, and none of them are trustworthy about what happens to a household's food data. If this is not solved, the status quo continues: choosing recipes and shopping for them stays a chore done badly or not at all, and the cost of "what shall we cook" keeps being paid in wasted food and repeated decisions.

A prior throwaway implementation of Pantry existed and was deleted deliberately so the project could be rebuilt through this spec-driven workflow. It demonstrated that the chain above can be closed end to end on-device; it also produced the constraints and cautions recorded below. This document scopes the rebuild, not a new idea.

## Target Users

### Recipe Collector (primary)

- **Role:** The same person in a different moment — browsing on a phone, finding a recipe worth keeping, wanting it captured before the tab is lost.
- **Needs:** To get a recipe off a web page and into a personal catalogue in a few seconds, from wherever they are browsing; to fix a bad import quickly rather than abandoning it, and to come back and fix or delete a recipe later if it turns out to need it; to enter a recipe by hand when there's no web page to import from at all; to trust that what was saved is what the page actually said; and later, to browse and compare what they've collected — by picture, by name, by season — well enough to decide what to cook without reopening every recipe.
- **Current Pain:** Bookmarks accumulate and are never revisited; pages go dead or get rewritten; screenshots are not searchable or usable as data. Retyping a recipe by hand is the only reliable option and nobody does it.

### Online Grocery Shopper (secondary)

- **Role:** The same person at the point of ordering, working through a finished shopping list against a supermarket's own website in their own logged-in session.
- **Needs:** To be walked through the list item by item without losing their place, landing on the retailer's own search results for each item, usable single-handed while distracted in-store and resumable after an interruption, and to keep full control over what actually goes in the basket and in what quantity.
- **Current Pain:** Typing every item into the retailer's search box, scrolling back to a list held elsewhere, and losing track of which items have been added. Full automation is not wanted here — product choice and quantity are judgement calls the shopper wants to keep.

## Goals

- **G1 — Capture, correct and maintain recipes accurately.** A recipe on a web page ends up in the local catalogue, accurate and editable, without the user transcribing it, and a bad parse is a quick correction rather than a dead end. A recipe can also be entered entirely by hand — title, ingredients, method — for a source with no URL at all. A saved recipe stays editable after it's been saved, not only in the moment of import, so a recipe that parsed poorly can be salvaged later rather than only during that first correction; a saved recipe can also be deleted from the catalogue.
- **G2 — Browse, search, filter and choose recipes without scheduling them.** The user can browse their saved recipes as cards carrying a thumbnail image, find one by searching its name or filtering by season status, and see enough information (cooking time, energy, seasonality) to choose between recipes without opening each one — and can put a chosen recipe straight into the shopping-list selection, with no day or meal slot to designate first.
- **G3 — One list, correctly combined.** Any selection of chosen recipes collapses into a single shopping list whose quantities are merged only where merging is physically valid, scaled to the servings actually being cooked, and ordered for the way a shop is walked (a stable sequence approximating a single front-to-back walk through a store's sections).
- **G4 — Honest numbers, not invented ones.** Per-recipe nutrition information is available where the underlying data supports it, and wherever a figure rests on an estimate or incomplete ingredient coverage the interface says so rather than presenting a confident number.
- **G5 — Faster shopping without automating the retailer.** Working the list against a retailer's website is materially faster than typing each item, while every basket action — product choice, quantity, checkout — remains a human action on the retailer's own page, and the app never claims knowledge of basket state it does not have.
- **G6 — Local-first and obligation-free.** The app is usable with no account, no API key, no backend service, and no network connection except for the network-dependent actions named elsewhere in this document — fetching a recipe from a URL (G1), looking up nutrition data for an ingredient (G4), and loading the retailer's own site in the in-app browser for retailer-assist (G5, this does not extend to the retailer's own login shown during retailer-assist) — and a user's recipes, selections and lists never leave the device.
- **G7 — Seasonal awareness that admits ignorance.** The user can see whether a recipe's ingredients are in season and get substitution suggestions when they are not, with unrecognised ingredients reported as unknown rather than guessed either way. Wherever the app summarises a whole recipe rather than one ingredient (e.g. a recipe card), that summary carries the same honesty: it distinguishes a recipe that's genuinely out of season from one that's merely partly unrecognised, rather than treating an unknown as a clean pass.
- **G8 — Ready to distribute responsibly.** Because Pantry is intended for distribution (Q2, resolved) rather than staying on the developer's own device, it meets baseline expectations for a user who isn't the developer: usable with a screen reader and at larger text sizes, honest with the app store and the user about what data it touches, and clear about what level of support a solo-developer app can actually offer.

## Non-Goals

- **Accounts, sync, sharing and any backend service.** No Pantry user accounts, no multi-user support, no cross-device sync, no server operated by this project. (This does not extend to the retailer's own login shown during retailer-assist — the user manages that entirely on the retailer's site, entirely outside Pantry's purview; see G5, G6, Constraints/Technology.) This is the constraint that makes the rest of the design possible; adding it later is a different project.
- **Programmatic basket filling or checkout automation.** The app will not scrape a retailer's page DOM, will not add items to a basket programmatically, will not drive the page at machine speed, and will never touch checkout or payment. Assisted navigation only, under the user's own session. This is a deliberate line, not a temporary limitation.
- **Verified basket reconciliation.** The app will not attempt to read, confirm or reconcile the true contents of a retailer basket. Its own progress-through-the-list tracking is a local convenience and must not be presented as a receipt.
- **Recipe suggestions driven by a retailer's discounted-item listing.** Identified in the prior build and never implemented. Excluded from this project's goals; it may be revisited as a later-phase candidate once the core chain works.
- **Diet, health and consumption tracking.** No logging of what was actually eaten, no weight or macro targets, no dietary or medical guidance. Nutrition figures here support recipe comparison, nothing more.
- **Nutritional or allergen accuracy guarantees.** Figures come from a bundled staples table and a third-party open food database; the project's obligation is disclosure of uncertainty (G4), not correctness of third-party data. The app is not a safe source of allergen information, and this applies equally to any substitution suggested for an out-of-season ingredient (G7) — a suggested substitute carries no allergen-safety guarantee either.
- **Platforms other than Android phone.** No iOS, no web, no desktop, no tablet- or wear-specific layouts.
- **Recipe authoring, publishing or community features.** No sharing, no ratings, no comments, no public recipe corpus — this does not exclude entering a recipe manually into the user's own private catalogue (G1), only publishing or sharing it with anyone else.
- **Offline recipe import.** Importing from a URL inherently requires the network; there is no scope item to work around this.
- **Locales other than the Republic of Ireland for seasonality data.** The growing-calendar reference (G7) is Republic-of-Ireland-specific by design. If the developer's own location or circumstances change, retargeting it is a re-scoping decision for a later phase, not a live constraint this document resolves.
- **Robust handling of non-recipe input.** A shared or pasted URL that is not `http(s)`, or a shared item that is not a web page, is rejected with a clear error rather than guessed at; the import path is not required to make sense of arbitrary attacker-supplied content beyond declining it safely.
- **Treating every fetched page as importable.** A fetched page carrying no extractable recipe content at all (no structured recipe data, and no heuristic fallback able to identify one) is declined with a clear error rather than turned into an empty draft for the user to fill in by hand. This is narrower than the non-`http(s)` exclusion above, which is about the *URL scheme* rather than the page's content. It is bounded to that one case — no extractable content whatsoever — precisely so it cannot become a second way for G1's recoverability promise to fail: any page from which *something* is extracted, however incomplete or low-confidence, is a recipe that parses imperfectly, not a rejected page, and stays on the always-recoverable editable-draft path (SC-G1).
- **Day/meal scheduling and weekly planning.** No calendar or week layout, no assigning a recipe to a day or a meal slot, no per-day energy or cooking-time rollups. Recipes go from browsing and selection directly to a shopping list; deciding *when* something gets cooked stays outside the app.
- **A specific image-selection algorithm.** This document commits only to a preference order among whatever image signals a source page offers — structured metadata (e.g. a recipe schema's own image field, or an `og:image` tag) before a bare inline `<img>` guess — not to a particular extraction implementation; the exact heuristic is an Architecture-phase decision.
- **Formal accessibility certification, a full WCAG audit, or a live support channel.** G8 commits to baseline accessibility (content descriptions, text-scaling support, non-color-only indicators, sufficient contrast) and best-effort, no-SLA support (a single named channel, e.g. a public issue tracker), not a certified audit or a staffed support desk — consistent with the solo-developer Team constraint.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] **(G1)** A recipe page shared into the app from a phone browser, or pasted in as a URL, produces a populated, editable draft — title, ingredients, method — that the user can correct and save without typing the recipe from scratch.
- [ ] **(G1)** An import of a page that *is* a recipe but parses badly is still recoverable by editing the draft; no such import path ends in a state where the user's only option is to abandon it.
- [ ] **(G1)** A fetched page from which no recipe content can be extracted at all (no structured recipe data and no heuristic fallback able to identify one) is declined with a clear, specific error saying the page was not recognised as a recipe — not silently turned into an empty editable draft. Any page from which *something* is extracted, however incomplete, is not this case — it is a recipe that parses badly and stays on the always-recoverable draft path (the preceding criterion), so the two criteria never compete for the same page. The rejection message is worded distinctly from the parse-badly case so the user can tell "this isn't a recipe" from "this is a recipe I read imperfectly," and the user is left able to try another URL without restarting the app.
- [ ] **(G1)** Saved recipes persist locally across app restarts and device reboots and are readable with the network off.
- [ ] **(G1)** A saved recipe imported from a URL retains that source URL and the time it was fetched, so the user can revisit the original page to check what was originally captured against it; a manually entered recipe (see below) has no source page to retain and is not expected to show one. This retained source/fetch-time pair describes the recipe's original capture and is not updated by a later post-save edit — a recipe salvaged or corrected after the fact still shows when and from where it was first captured, not when it was last edited.
- [ ] **(G1)** Capturing a recipe via share-into-app or paste-URL takes on the order of seconds of user interaction, not minutes — verified informally by the developer's own comparison against manual retyping, not a formal benchmark.
- [ ] **(G1)** The user can create a recipe entirely by hand — the same optional fields an import can carry (cooking time, servings/yield, a thumbnail image) are available too — without pasting or sharing any URL. A thumbnail for a manually entered recipe comes from the device's own photo picker, since there is no source page to fetch one from. Ingredient lines typed by hand are parsed by the same mechanism that reads an imported recipe's ingredient lines, so G3's merging, G4's nutrition matching and G7's seasonality lookups work identically regardless of how the recipe was captured. The saved recipe sits in the same local catalogue as an imported one, indistinguishable in every downstream feature (browsing, filtering, selection, shopping-list generation) except for having no source URL or fetch time.
- [ ] **(G1)** Any saved recipe — imported or manually entered — can be opened for editing at any later time from its own detail screen, not only in the moment it was first captured, so a recipe that parsed badly (or was entered with a mistake) can be corrected once discovered rather than only during the original import's draft step. The same device photo picker used for manual entry lets the user attach or replace the thumbnail on any saved recipe during such an edit, so a bad or missing import-fetched thumbnail isn't stuck once saved. Title and at least one ingredient line are required whenever a recipe is saved, whichever path produced it — manual entry, an import draft, or a later edit of either.
- [ ] **(G1)** Any saved recipe can be deleted from the catalogue from its own detail screen. This is a single two-stage action, not a confirmation dialog followed by a separate undo mechanism: tapping delete removes the recipe from view immediately (it disappears from the catalogue and, if it was part of the shopping-list selection, from there too, with a visible notice per G2), and an undo option (e.g. "Recipe deleted — Undo") stays available for a brief window long enough for a screen-reader or assistive-tech user to react to, not a fixed cosmetic duration. Tapping Undo within that window restores the recipe and its selection membership exactly as they were, with nothing having been visibly lost. Only once the window elapses without Undo does the underlying data actually get purged — a silent, already-invisible-to-the-user finalization, since the app provides no sync, backend, or restore feature of its own to recover it afterward (Non-Goals, G6's local-only design). If the app process is killed or the app isn't returned to before that finalization would have happened, the pending deletion is discarded instead of silently completing in the background, and the recipe reappears on next launch — the safer of the two possible outcomes for an otherwise-unrecoverable action.
- [ ] **(G2)** The user can browse and compare candidate recipes as cards, each card showing cooking time, energy content (kcal) and seasonality status without opening the recipe individually, using the aggregate seasonality states defined under Success Criteria (G7). Where any nutrition figure shown is estimated or incomplete per G4's disclosure rule, a single caveat indicator makes that visible on the card too — the full breakdown (which figures are estimated, what proportion of ingredients matched) stays one tap away on the recipe's own detail screen rather than being spelled out on the card. The seasonality-status indicator and the nutrition-caveat indicator are visually distinct from each other and from the no-caveat state, so a glancing comparison can't mistake one kind of "not sure" for the other. A recipe with no stated cooking time (a manually entered recipe need not give one, per G1) shows an explicit no-time state on its card rather than a blank or invented figure, the same absence-handling already required for a missing thumbnail; energy content has no equivalent gap, since G4 always produces either a figure or a disclosed caveat, never a blank.
- [ ] **(G2)** Each card also shows a thumbnail image where the source page offered one usable under the preference order named in Non-Goals; the image is fetched and stored locally as part of the same import fetch that captures the rest of the recipe (G1), not loaded live from the source page afterward — thumbnails do not add a fourth network-dependent action beyond the three named in G6. Where no usable image was available, the card shows an explicit no-image state rather than a placeholder that could be mistaken for the recipe's own picture — an absent image is shown as absent, never guessed or substituted.
- [ ] **(G2)** The user can filter the recipe catalogue two ways, independently and combinably: by typing part of a recipe's name, and by seasonality status (in season / out of season / partial — the three states defined under G7, selectable together or separately). Clearing the name search removes only that filter — the view returns to whatever the season filter alone would show (the full catalogue if no season filter is active), never clearing a season filter the user didn't touch.
- [ ] **(G2)** Each saved recipe is viewable on its own detail screen showing its full ingredients, method and the nutrition breakdown referenced above.
- [ ] **(G2)** A recipe can be added to (and removed from) the shopping-list selection directly from the card/listing view or from its own detail screen, with no day or meal assignment required at any point, and the user can go from that selection to a generated shopping list (G3) without an intervening scheduling step. Adding a recipe to the selection lets the user set the servings to shop for, defaulting to the recipe's own stated yield where one was given; a recipe with no stated yield (permitted for a manually entered recipe, per G1) defaults instead to treating its quantities as already correct for the servings on the recipe as saved — a 1× scale factor the user can still change — rather than inventing a serving count. This per-selection serving figure is what G3's scaling criterion operates on. The shopping-list selection persists locally across app restarts, the same as saved recipes (G1) and the retailer-assist walkthrough's position (G5).
- [ ] **(G2)** If a recipe in the shopping-list selection is later edited or deleted from the catalogue before a shopping list is generated from it, the selection reflects the change (updated nutrition/time, or the recipe dropped with a visible notice) rather than silently showing stale data; a serving figure the user already set for that selection entry is preserved across the edit, since it is the user's own choice for this shop rather than a copy of the recipe's default yield. Once a shopping list has been generated (G3), it is a snapshot as of that moment — a later edit or deletion of a source recipe does not retroactively change an already-generated list.
- [ ] **(G2)** When a fetched page is declined as not a recipe (per the Non-Goals exception to G1), the user can optionally share or export that page's URL for the developer's own later inspection, entirely at the user's initiative — this is the only feedback path for a possible misclassification, consistent with G6's no-telemetry design; the app itself neither collects nor transmits anything about the decline on its own.
- [ ] **(G3)** Selecting multiple recipes produces one list in which the same ingredient appearing in several recipes is combined into a single entry.
- [ ] **(G3)** Quantities are combined only where the units are physically comparable (e.g. 1 kg and 500 g of the same ingredient combine to 1.5 kg), and non-comparable quantities of a nominally similar ingredient stay as separate entries (e.g. 400 g tomatoes and 2 tbsp tomato purée).
- [ ] **(G3)** An ingredient whose quantity cannot be parsed or compared appears on the list marked as unquantified rather than carrying a number the app invented.
- [ ] **(G3)** Changing the servings for a recipe scales its contributed quantities proportionally on the resulting list (a 4-serving recipe cooked for 6 contributes 1.5×); a recipe with no stated yield scales from the 1× baseline described under G2 instead.
- [ ] **(G3)** List entries are grouped by supermarket section in a stable, walk-ordered sequence rather than recipe order.
- [ ] **(G4)** A recipe with recognised ingredients shows per-recipe nutrition figures.
- [ ] **(G4)** Wherever a figure depends on an estimated weight conversion, the interface labels it as estimated; wherever ingredients were unmatched, the interface discloses what proportion of the recipe's ingredients contributed real data.
- [ ] **(G4)** An unmatched ingredient contributes nothing to the totals — the app never substitutes a guessed value for missing data.
- [ ] **(G4)** Nutrition figures sourced from the external food-data lookup are attributed to that source somewhere the user can see, per its usage terms; if the source ultimately selected carries a share-alike or similar obligation for a locally stored derived dataset (a live possibility with open food-data sources generally, and true of Open Food Facts' ODbL specifically, though the source itself remains undecided — see Constraints/Technology), that obligation is met, not just the on-screen credit. The concrete compliance mechanism is an Architecture-phase decision; this criterion commits only to meeting whatever the selected source's license actually requires. Because Pantry is distributed (Q2, resolved), any such share-alike obligation applies in full rather than being contingent on the distribution path.
- [ ] **(G5)** From a finished shopping list the user can step through items one at a time, each step landing on the retailer's own search results page for that item inside the in-app browser view, and can advance, skip, or go back without losing their place.
- [ ] **(G5)** The app issues no programmatic add-to-basket, no DOM manipulation of the retailer's page, and no navigation to checkout; every basket change originates from a human tap on the retailer's own controls. Verifiable by inspection of the implementation and by observing that the app's own controls only move a local queue pointer.
- [ ] **(G5)** No interface text asserts that an item is in the retailer's basket. Progress wording refers to the user's position in the local list only.
- [ ] **(G5)** The user's position in the retailer-assist walkthrough survives the app being backgrounded or the process being killed and restarted — resuming lands back on the same list position, not the start.
- [ ] **(G5)** If the user is not already logged into the retailer's site, the in-app browser shows the retailer's own login flow exactly as a normal visit to that site would; Pantry stores no retailer credentials and does not manage retailer authentication. Because Pantry is distributed to users who didn't build it (G8), the retailer's page is shown through a mechanism that gives the user a verifiable indicator of the real domain and connection security (e.g. Android Custom Tabs' address bar), not a bare WebView with no way to check where the login form actually is.
- [ ] **(G5)** The retailer-assist walkthrough's own controls (advance, skip, back) are reachable and operable one-handed, with no multi-finger gesture required, matching the persona's need to use them while distracted in-store.
- [ ] **(G5)** Stepping through a shopping list of the size named in the Problem Statement (around 30 items) via retailer-assist takes measurably fewer taps and actions per item than typing each item into the retailer's search box by hand — verified informally by the developer's own comparison, not a formal benchmark.
- [ ] **(G6)** With the network disabled, every function except recipe-URL import, nutrition lookup, and retailer-assist browsing remains usable: browsing, searching, filtering, manually creating, editing and deleting recipes, selecting recipes, list generation, and the shopping list itself.
- [ ] **(G6)** The app itself requires no sign-in, stores no credentials, and ships no API key (this does not extend to the retailer's own login shown during retailer-assist, per G5 — Pantry stores and manages none of it); it can be installed on a clean device and used immediately.
- [ ] **(G6)** No recipe, selection or shopping-list data is transmitted anywhere. The only outbound network activity is the user-initiated recipe fetch, the ingredient nutrition lookup, and the retailer-assist browser loading pages from the retailer's own site — none of it carries Pantry's own data to a Pantry-operated destination, because none exists.
- [ ] **(G7)** A recipe's ingredients can be checked against an Irish (Republic of Ireland) growing calendar, and out-of-season ingredients are identified with at least one substitution suggestion where one exists.
- [ ] **(G7)** An ingredient absent from the seasonality reference is reported as unknown and is not presented as either in or out of season.
- [ ] **(G7)** A recipe's own aggregate seasonality status (used wherever the app summarises a whole recipe rather than one ingredient, e.g. the G2 browse/compare view) is exactly one of three states, evaluated in this order so the states never overlap: **out of season** first — at least one recognised ingredient is currently out of season, regardless of any unknowns; otherwise **partial** — no recognised ingredient is out of season, but at least one ingredient is unrecognised; otherwise **in season** — every ingredient is recognised and in season. Out-of-season and unknown are never merged into the same displayed state, and the ordering means "in season" is the residual case, never co-satisfied with "partial."
- [ ] **(G7)** Wherever a substitution is suggested for an out-of-season ingredient, the no-allergen-safety-guarantee disclaimer (see Non-Goals) is visible alongside it, not only on nutrition figures.
- [ ] **(G8)** Interactive elements carry accessible content descriptions and the interface respects the system's font-scaling setting, so the app is usable with a screen reader and at larger text sizes — verified before submission by exercising the core flow with a screen reader and an enlarged text size enabled, not asserted without a check.
- [ ] **(G8)** No indicator anywhere in the app (including the G2 seasonality/nutrition-caveat indicators and the G5 retailer-assist walkthrough's progress) is conveyed by color alone; each is also distinguishable by shape, icon, or text.
- [ ] **(G8)** The G5 retailer-assist walkthrough's progress (e.g. "item 5 of 30") is conveyed in text a screen reader can read, not only by position on screen, and its controls meet basic glance-legibility (sufficient contrast and text size for a quick, distracted look), building on the one-handed-operability criterion already in G5.
- [ ] **(G8)** A first-run experience orients a new user — not just the developer — to the core flow (import a recipe or enter one by hand, choose recipes, generate a shopping list) without requiring external instructions.
- [ ] **(G8)** The app launches and completes its core flow without crashing on at least one device other than the developer's own primary device (a second physical device or an emulator profile the developer doesn't normally develop against), verified before submission.
